Strasbourg Museums: Which Are Worth Your Time

One of Strasbourg's best-known museums is closed for restoration until 1 June 2028. Yet travel round-ups and ready-made itineraries still recommend it without mentioning the closure.

By Eugene · Updated November 2026 · 11-minute read

Strasbourg has around ten city museums, but trying to see them all in one trip isn't a good idea. If you're here for two days, it makes more sense to pick two, three at most: that leaves time for the city itself. Luckily, some of the most interesting museums are right next to the cathedral, so they're easy to combine with a walk around the historic centre.

The Alsatian Museum: a wait until 2028

The Alsatian Museum (Musée Alsacien) is closed for restoration until 1 June 2028. That matters because it's still one of the museums most often recommended to first-time visitors. Inside are reconstructed Alsatian rooms and workshops. Furniture, crockery, embroidered bedspreads and toys show how ordinary people lived here in the 18th and 19th centuries, rather than their bishops.

When the museum reopens, it's worth giving it at least an hour. For now, you'll have to make do with admiring the building from outside. If you come across an itinerary suggesting you go inside right now, you'll know its author hasn't checked the opening times in a while.

What it costs and when a day pass pays off

A full-price ticket to most city museums costs €9. There are exceptions: the Aubette 1928 interiors are free, and the Heitz gallery has its own price. Admission to the city museums is free for under-18s. On the first Sunday of the month, everyone gets in free.

If you plan to visit several museums in a day, compare the day passes first. There are two, and each costs €20. One covers the city museums, so you can easily combine, say, the Cathedral Museum, the Historical Museum and one of the Palais Rohan museums. The other is for the Palais Rohan itself, where the Museum of Fine Arts, the Museum of Decorative Arts and the Archaeological Museum share one roof. It also includes the Heitz gallery. As both cost the same, check at the ticket desk which one suits your plans better before you buy.

For comparison, three separate tickets to the palace museums come to €27. So a day pass pays off if you're planning a full museum day. If you only want to drop into one or two museums, ordinary tickets are usually enough.

Single tickets and day passes are bought at the museum ticket desk: there are no official online sales. On weekdays when the museums are open, many of them close for lunch from 13:00 to 14:00. At weekends most stay open without a lunch break.

It's also worth doing the maths on the Strasbourg City Card. It costs €5, is valid for seven days and gives discounts on museums, the cathedral viewing platform and the Batorama boat trip. You can buy it online, but you'll need to collect it from the tourist office on Place de la Cathédrale. If you're going to climb the cathedral and take a cruise on the Ill anyway, the card will most likely pay for itself. Five museums worth your time

1. The Cathedral Museum (Musée de l'Œuvre Notre-Dame)

€9 · about an hour and a quarter · the perfect partner to the cathedral.

This is where the original medieval sculptures that once adorned the cathedral are kept. Much of what you photograph on the façade is a copy, while the originals stand here at eye level, away from the weather that was destroying them. Among them are the Church and the Synagogue and the famous Tempter. Seeing a 13th-century face from forty centimetres away rather than forty metres changes the way you look at the cathedral itself. Nearby are works by Nikolaus Gerhaert of Leyden, Konrad Witz and Hans Baldung Grien.

The museum is one of Strasbourg's city museums. It shouldn't be confused with the Fondation de l'Œuvre Notre-Dame, which has existed since the Middle Ages and is still responsible for looking after the building. The foundation's stonemasons still work the pink Vosges sandstone and restore the cathedral's sculpted decoration.

Who will love it: anyone who has spent more than five minutes in front of the façade. Best visited on the same day as Strasbourg Cathedral.

2. The Strasbourg Historical Museum (Musée Historique)

€9 · about an hour · the city's history in the former Grande Boucherie.

The museum occupies the Grande Boucherie, the great meat hall built in 1587–1588. Meat was sold here, and there was a slaughterhouse in the inner courtyard. Nothing inside reminds you of that today.

The galleries display armour, old weapons and townspeople's costumes. But the museum's real treasure is the huge relief model of Strasbourg, made in the 18th century. Spend twenty minutes in front of it, and the streets you know start to form a clear picture. You see how the river wraps around the Grande Île, where the fortifications ran and why the Ponts Couverts were built exactly where they are. The island itself is covered in detail in our guide to Strasbourg Old Town.

Who will love it: anyone who likes to understand where they are, and children who enjoy models and old objects: the displays include play and digital stations. With very young children, it's worth estimating in advance how long they'll last.

3. Palais Rohan: the Museum of Fine Arts

€9 or a day pass · about an hour · the most interesting of the palace's three museums for painting lovers.

Half the experience is the palace itself. This 18th-century residence was built for Cardinal Armand-Gaston de Rohan, who, according to local legend, was an illegitimate son of Louis XIV. The grandeur has a simpler explanation, though: the palace was designed as a stately residence for the prince-bishop, fit to receive the king and other distinguished guests. Louis XV, Marie Antoinette and Napoleon all stayed here.

The collection covers European painting from the early Italian masters to the 19th century, including works by Giotto and Rubens. It isn't the Louvre, but it's a genuine collection in rooms where it's a pleasure to linger.

Who will love it: lovers of painting and 18th-century interiors. Who should skip it: anyone with three hours for the whole of Strasbourg.

4. Palais Rohan: the Museum of Decorative Arts

€9 or a day pass · 40 minutes · the star exhibit is the state apartments.

The Museum of Decorative Arts occupies part of the same palace, but it needs a separate ticket. The main draw is the cardinal's state apartments, though the collection also includes ceramics and clocks. If historic interiors are what interest you most, forty minutes will be enough. Of the palace's three museums, this is the easiest to skip.

5. Palais Rohan: the Archaeological Museum

€9 or a day pass · about 45 minutes · in the cellars, and more interesting than it sounds.

Here you can trace the history of Alsace from prehistoric settlements to Roman Argentoratum and the early Middle Ages. It's especially interesting to see finds from the time when a Roman military camp stood where Strasbourg is today. Who should skip it: anyone who wouldn't stop at a Roman collection back home. If you only have one day, cross this museum off first.

Five more museums for different interests

  • Aubette 1928. Free avant-garde interiors on Place Kléber. Sophie Taeuber-Arp, Hans Arp and Theo van Doesburg turned the café and halls here into a complete modernist work of art. A good choice if you'd like to see a Strasbourg that isn't medieval at all. Usually open Wednesday to Saturday, 14:00–18:00.
  • The Zoological Museum. Natural history in the university quarter, off the island. After a major renovation it reopened in 2025 and now tells the story of biodiversity and humanity's relationship with nature. A good choice for families and natural history fans, but it won't do much for those who came for the half-timbering.
  • Le Vaisseau. An interactive science museum away from the historic centre. If you're travelling with children under 12, especially in rainy weather, it's worth a visit. It isn't a city museum, so neither the day pass nor the free first Sunday applies. Adults without children usually find the city museums more interesting.
  • The Tomi Ungerer Museum. Ungerer was born in Strasbourg and used the same pen for children's book illustrations and biting political satire. The displays show both sides of his work without trying to smooth the rough edges. You can also see work by other 20th- and 21st-century illustrators here. Small and sharp, about 45 minutes, and the best place in the city for anyone who loves illustration.
  • The Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art (MAMCS). That glass building you can see from the Barrage Vauban terrace. It's one of Strasbourg's main art museums, with works from the late 19th century to the present day. If modern art interests you, allow a good hour and a half. If your trip is short and the medieval city is your priority, it can wait until next time.

When it makes sense to skip the museums

If you have one day, the answer is one museum or none. The best of Strasbourg is outdoors and free: the cathedral's west front, the Ponts Couverts, the Barrage Vauban terrace, all gathered in our list of things to do in Strasbourg. The One day in Strasbourg plan leaves exactly one slot for a museum, after lunch.

Arriving on a Monday? Drop museums from your plan, except the three in the Palais Rohan. A boat trip, the cathedral climb and a walk through the Neustadt make good substitutes. On Tuesday it's the other way round: that's when the Palais Rohan is closed.

On the first Sunday of the month the city museums are free, so you won't need a day pass for them. Get to the Cathedral Museum when it opens at 10:00.

Ready to explore Strasbourg?

In Strasbourg you can easily spend a whole day without setting foot in a museum, and it won't be time wasted. The cathedral façade changes with the light, old houses are reflected in the canals of Petite France, and the Barrage Vauban terrace looks out over the roofs of the historic centre. The city itself is like a museum, just without walls or tickets.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best museum in Strasbourg?

For a first visit, the Cathedral Museum (Musée de l'Œuvre Notre-Dame). It holds the original medieval sculptures that once adorned the cathedral façade, where copies stand today. Allow about an hour and a quarter, tickets €9.

How much do Strasbourg museums cost?

A full-price ticket to most city museums costs €9. A day pass costs €20 and comes in two versions: for the city museums and for the three Palais Rohan museums. Under-18s get in free, and on the first Sunday of the month the city museums are free for everyone.

Is a day pass worth it?

Yes, if you want to visit three main paid museums at full price in one day: three separate tickets cost €27, the pass €20. For one or two museums, ordinary tickets are usually enough.

Can I buy museum tickets online?

No. Single tickets and day passes are bought at the museum ticket desk, and there are no official online sales.

Is the Alsatian Museum open?

No. It's closed for restoration until 1 June 2028, although it still appears in guides and itineraries that nobody has updated.

When are Strasbourg museums closed?

Most city museums close on Mondays, and the three Palais Rohan museums on Tuesdays. Many open from 10:00 to 13:00 and 14:00 to 18:00 on weekdays, and from 10:00 to 18:00 at weekends. Some sites, including Aubette 1928, have different hours.

Which museum is best with children?

Le Vaisseau, an interactive science museum away from the historic centre, worth half a day. Children will also enjoy the renovated Zoological Museum and the relief model in the Historical Museum.
