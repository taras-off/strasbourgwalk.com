Strasbourg in Winter: Weather, Crowds and What to Do Beyond the Market

In recent years the chalets have been gone by December 25. The lights are still strung across the streets, the cathedral is still floodlit, and the room that cost a fortune on December 20 is suddenly affordable. That week usually gets overlooked amid the Christmas rush, which is exactly what makes it interesting.

If you are still choosing when to go to Strasbourg, winter is the trickiest season to plan. It is not one season but three different trips: the market weeks, the quiet stretch after Christmas Eve, and the gray, cold stretch of January and February. People who leave disappointed have usually picked the wrong one.

The first is mulled wine, crowds at the stalls, and the Strasbourg Christmas market in all its glory. The other two have a different mood altogether: the lights without the crowd, half-empty museum galleries, long lunches in a warm winstub. Winter here is three different trips

December, up to the 24th

Everything you have read about Strasbourg at Christmas applies to these four weeks and only to them. Around Place Kléber on a December Saturday the crowd barely moves.

The market is especially atmospheric in the evening, when the lights come on over the chalets, so spend the daylight on the city and head for the stalls once it gets dark.

The last week of December and early January

Very few people plan a trip for this week. The stalls are gone after Christmas, but what made it feel festive stays: the strings of lights, the lit shop windows, the tree on Place Kléber, the floodlit sandstone. The decorations usually stay up into early January, while the crowd that came for them has already left.

Prices usually follow the crowd down. Rooms that were hard to find in mid-December tend to reappear between Christmas and New Year. Look up hotel rates for December 18, December 27, and January 4, and the difference shows at once.

Two warnings. On December 25 and January 1 much of the city shuts down: the cathedral platform, the city museums, and many restaurants are closed on both days. The city decides each year when to take the lights down, usually in early January. So treat them as a bonus, not a reason to pick your dates.

January and February

Cold, gray, cheap, and empty. Whether that is a warning or an invitation depends on what you came for.

The upsides of January and February are almost empty museum galleries on weekday mornings, winstub tables that are usually easier to get without a reservation, and the cathedral in the low winter sun, when the pink sandstone shifts from gray toward red within an hour.

But the quiet has another side. Petite France in the rain at four in the afternoon, with canals the color of dishwater, is not exactly postcard material. There is no market, no festive lighting after the first days of January, and no café terraces. If the Strasbourg you want is all about walking outdoors, come another month. If you come for the cathedral, the museums, and the food, January and February are among the best-value times to visit.

Strasbourg is also a parliamentary city, and session weeks fill hotels regardless of the season. The European Parliament sits in Strasbourg on December 14–17, 2026, January 18–21, 2027, and February 15–18, 2027, and the Council of Europe's Parliamentary Assembly meets on January 25–29, 2027. Before you book, check that your dates do not overlap one of these sessions.

The fastest trains from Paris reach Strasbourg in about 1 hour 45 minutes, and outside the busiest December dates, cheaper fares are usually easier to find. Before you buy, check SNCF times and fares.

The weather

Winter here is gray and cold rather than rainy. The Vosges, west of the city, put Alsace in a marked rain shadow, so Strasbourg stays fairly dry in winter: December brings about 45 mm of precipitation, and January and February less than that. The real catch is the lack of sunshine: December averages only about 44 hours of sunshine in the whole month. On still mornings, fog can form along the Ill, and temperatures drop below freezing on about half the nights in each winter month.

How cold it feels depends on what you are doing. Walking around the Grande Île in a warm coat is fine for hours. What really gets to you is standing still for a long time: at a market stall, in a line, or on the cathedral platform 66 meters up. Dress for long stops, with a hat, gloves, a scarf, and waterproof shoes with good traction, because wet cobblestones in the dark are slippery. A hood works better than an umbrella in the crowded streets.

Daylight shapes your day more than the temperature does

In mid-December, it is light from a little after 8:00 until about 16:30. Eight hours of daylight reshape the whole rhythm of the day, so it pays to keep daytime and evening plans apart.

Save the cathedral platform, a boat on the Ill, Petite France, the covered bridges, and the terrace on the Vauban dam for daylight. Keep the market, the floodlit facades, and dinner in a winstub for the evening. Get the timing wrong, and you'll climb 330 steps just to look at streetlights.

The cathedral is built of pink sandstone, and its color changes with the light. In January the cathedral looks its best in the last hour before sunset, so plan to be in front of the west facade then. Low sun picks out the portals and the hundreds of stone figures that make Strasbourg Cathedral worth taking your time with.

A plan for a cold, wet day

On a day like this, stick to places with a roof. This itinerary works best from Wednesday to Sunday: the Historical Museum is closed on Mondays and the Palais Rohan museums on Tuesdays. This itinerary won't work on December 25 or January 1.

10:00 — Palais Rohan. Built in the 18th century for Strasbourg's prince-bishops, the palace next to the cathedral now houses three museums: fine arts, decorative arts, and archaeology. The Fine Arts Museum alone, with Giotto, Raphael, and Rubens, can easily take an hour. If you plan to see three museums or more, ask at the desk about the one-day pass, which covers all the city museums, and check the current price on the museums' website.

12:00 — the Historical Museum, a few minutes' walk away. Its 1727 relief map of the city, covering about 70 square meters, explains the layout of the island under your feet faster than any map on a phone. On weekdays, remember the 13:00–14:00 break.

13:00 — lunch in a winstub. Take your time over it.

14:30 — a covered boat on the Ill. Batorama's tour lasts about 70 minutes aboard covered, heated boats. In winter the last boats depart much earlier than in summer, so check that day's timetable and buy your ticket in the morning rather than when the rain starts. Tickets are sold on the Batorama website, at its shop on Place de la Cathédrale, and from ticket machines by the shop and at the docks.

16:00 — the cathedral. Free, indoors, and its 12th- to 14th-century stained glass looks its best in the soft, even winter light. The nave closes at 17:45, so you have time before dinner.

What winter does to the food

This is the season Alsatian cooking was built for. Almost everything on a winstub menu is a slow, hearty answer to cold weather: choucroute garnie, baeckeoffe (the name means "baker's oven"), flammekueche, Munster cheese with caraway and potatoes, and kougelhopf with coffee. Pair it all with a local white wine in a green-stemmed glass. And that is only the winter part of what to eat in Strasbourg.

In winter all of this is best eaten at a table in a warm winstub. And in January and February a table at 20:00 is usually easier to find than during the market weeks.

Ready for a winter walk?

Come right after Christmas or in late January, keep the daylight for the city, and save the evening for the lights.

Frequently asked questions

What is the weather like in Strasbourg in December?

Cold and gray, with average highs around 6°C (43°F) and nights near freezing. It is not especially rainy, but December gets only about 44 hours of sunshine in the whole month, and still mornings can be foggy.

Is Strasbourg worth visiting in January?

Yes, if you come for the cathedral, the museums, and the food rather than the atmosphere outdoors. January hotel rates are usually noticeably lower than in December and the museums are quiet, but there is no market and the festive lights come down early in the month.

Is the Christmas market open after Christmas?

In recent years, no: the main market has closed on December 24. For 2026 the city has so far confirmed only the opening date, November 27, so check the closing date in the official program. The festive lights usually stay up into early January.

What time does it get dark in Strasbourg in winter?

Around 16:30 in mid-December, with sunrise a little after 8:00, so you get about eight hours of daylight. Keep the platform, the boat ride, and Petite France for daylight, and the floodlit facades for after dark.

Is Strasbourg cheaper in winter?

It depends on the week. Until December 24 hotels are much more expensive than usual. From December 25 prices usually fall sharply, and in January and February hotels are noticeably cheaper than in December. The exception is parliamentary session weeks.

Are the museums open in winter?

Yes. The three Palais Rohan museums close on Tuesdays, and the Historical Museum and most other city museums on Mondays. On weekdays they all take a break from 13:00 to 14:00. The city museums are closed on December 25 and January 1. Entry is free for everyone on the first Sunday of the month. The Musée Alsacien is closed until June 1, 2028.
